Who he is

Maurice Driz is one of the twin brothers at the center of this case, the father of co-defendant Shawn Driz, and, with his twin Alberto, a former owner of a chain of Blaze Pizza franchises. The family's money runs back a generation: their father, Mario Driz, founded a Los Angeles knitwear company, Driz Knitting Mills, and in the late 1980s Maurice and Alberto ran a British hosiery venture called Cosy Legs as its overseas arm.

The criminal case: convicted of felony assault

The 2015 attack outside The Nice Guy was prosecuted as a crime. In the Los Angeles criminal case (No. BA436130, at the downtown criminal courthouse), Maurice Driz was convicted of felony assault by means of force likely to produce great bodily injury (Penal Code 245(a)(4)) and sentenced. For his defense he retained a heavyweight team of private lawyers, led by Robert Shapiro, the attorney best known for defending O.J. Simpson, along with Mark Werksman and Tarek Shawky. The case was prosecuted by the Los Angeles County District Attorney's office.

The family business: Blaze Pizza, and a harassment verdict

The twins operated their Blaze Pizza franchises through a holding company and a stack of per-location LLCs. In a separate lawsuit, a former employee who was hired at age 16 won a jury verdict of roughly $492,000 against the Driz Blaze operation for sexual harassment and retaliation; Maurice was named among her supervisors. It is one more record of how this family ran its business.

Before Blaze: the Cosy Legs collapse

Cosy Legs, the twins' hosiery company, boomed in the late 1980s, winning a reported £3 million contract to supply the Soviet Union with three million pairs of woolly tights, and later ran a factory in England supplying major British retailers. Both arms of the business were wound up in 1993 and 1994, according to notices in the official government gazettes. It is background, but it fits a pattern of ventures that end in collapse.

Where his share stands

In July 2021 an L.A. jury ordered Maurice and his co-defendants to pay $72,645,492 to the three people they attacked. Roughly $12.8 million of the punitive damages was assessed against Maurice, and he is jointly liable for the whole judgment. Under California's 10% statutory interest, the debt has grown toward $122 million, and the victims have collected almost none of it. He represented himself at the civil trial.
