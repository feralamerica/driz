The Driz Judgment

Three people stepped in to stop a beating. A jury said the four men who put them in the hospital owe $72,645,492. Years later, the victims have collected almost nothing... and Anne Greene, who lost the most, is fighting off homelessness.

Still UnpaidAlmost a Decade Later

On April 17, 2015, Anne Greene, her brother Matthew, and their friend Jeremy Findel were outside The Nice Guy in West Hollywood when a group of men, after berating a valet, turned on two photographers. The three of them stepped in to stop it. For that, Anne left with a fractured skull and jaw and a traumatic brain injury that ended her career; Matthew's spine was fractured; Findel suffered a brain injury of his own.

In July 2021, after a three-week trial in which all four attackers represented themselves, a jury rejected their self-defense story and ordered them to pay $72,645,492, reported as one of the largest awards in the country for injuries that are predominantly PTSD. Nearly a decade later, the victims have collected almost none of it, and the men who owe it have spent years moving money and running out the clock. This is the record of who they are, what a jury found, and how they have tried to escape it. Every claim links to a document.

The dodge, in brief

The victims won on paper and then watched the money vanish. Shawn Driz filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy at 4:45 p.m. the night before trial, hoping to freeze the case. It failed: the court lifted the stay, the trial went ahead to the full verdict, and a debt for a willful, malicious injury is the kind bankruptcy cannot erase.

His uncle Alberto Driz had moved earlier. Two days after the Greenes filed suit in 2016, and the same day Blaze Pizza announced internally that "Daniel Driz, his brother, and his nephew have been removed from all Blaze Pizza operations," Alberto transferred his 49% stake in the family's Blaze holding company to Peter Koral in a below-market sale. The Greenes are now suing to unwind it, and a judge has let that fraudulent-transfer case proceed.

The judgment is final, and the Greenes are still in court to enforce it, through debtor examinations and levy fights that run into late 2026. A jury said Anne is owed. She still has not been paid.
